Research Interests

My research is grounded in formal methods and software engineering, focusing on the formal modeling and verification of security and privacy risks arising in data disclosure and de-identification processes at the design stage. With an emphasis on trustworthy and secure software systems, I systematically analyze re-identification risks and privacy–utility trade-offs in data and information infrastructures, including healthcare data and AI-enabled systems. Through this work, I aim to contribute verifiable data governance and system design methodologies that support the development of Next-Generation Digital Infrastructure.

Related: Trustworthy and Secure Software Systems / Formal Methods and Software Engineering / Data and Information Infrastructure / Security and Privacy in AI-enabled Systems / Next-Generation Digital Infrastructure

PhD Thesis (Working Title)

A Formal Framework for Analyzing Re-identification Risks in Healthcare Data

* Working title

Methodology

In this study, we use Alloy to formally model inter-dataset correlations using relational logic. Through model checking, we develop a verification tool that assesses, at the design stage, whether specific combinations of data releases logically permit re-identification.
